**Not in the usual sense.** The federal government borrows in dollars, the currency it issues. A country that borrows in someone else's currency can run out of that currency and be forced to miss payments. The US can't be forced into that position.

That doesn't make the debt risk-free. The risks are different ones:

- **The debt ceiling.** Congress sets a legal limit on how much the Treasury may borrow in total, separate from the spending and tax laws that create the need to borrow. If the limit is reached and not raised or suspended, the Treasury could be unable to pay every bill on time even though investors are willing to lend. That would be a default caused by a legal limit, not by inability to pay.
- **Interest costs.** As the debt grows and interest rates rise, more of the budget goes to interest, which leaves less room for everything else (Chart 3b).
- **Inflation.** A government that borrows in its own currency can always pay in dollars, but if it relied on creating money to do so, the cost would show up as higher prices rather than as missed payments.
- **Demand for Treasuries.** The US borrows cheaply partly because Treasuries are the world's main safe asset and the dollar its main reserve currency. If demand from investors at home or abroad weakened, borrowing would cost more (Chart 3c).
